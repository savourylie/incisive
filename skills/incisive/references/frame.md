# Playbook: frame

Craft the three highest-leverage surfaces that shape how readers interpret and remember the essay: the **introduction**, the **conclusion**, and the **title**. Run this only after the body argument is solid.

Load `references/calibration-examples.md` for framing and two-part title anchors.

## Part 1: Effective Introduction

Rewrite the introduction so it creates a compelling conceptual frame without falling into amateur openers:

- **Eliminate the 4 cliché openers**:
  1. Echoing the prompt/assignment verbatim without an original angle.
  2. Narrating the writer's personal thinking history.
  3. Citing a generic dictionary definition (if defining a key concept, engage a specific thinker's or domain specialist's definition to contest, refine, or extend it).
  4. Opening with a grandly banal platitude ("Throughout history...", "X is the greatest...").
- **Build the Puzzle Frame**:
  - Establish a brief shared context, then pivot sharply to what is puzzling, overlooked, contradictory, or commonly misread.
  - Optional hook: Open with a striking brief quotation or concrete anomaly that motivates the puzzle (which can later be echoed at the close of the conclusion).
  - End with the main claim (`point-first`) or the anticipatory launching sentence (`point-last`).

## Part 2: Effective Conclusion

Go beyond mechanical restatement:

1. **Anchor the Claim**: State or restate the matured main claim clearly near the start of the conclusion (usually sentence 1 or 2).
2. **Pass the "So What?" Test**: Explain the wider stakes or consequences of solving this puzzle, OR articulate the sharper, larger question the argument now opens up.
3. **Graceful Close (Optional)**: End with a concise, resonant image, concrete takeaway, or aphoristic quotation that leaves the core tension vivid.

## Part 3: Two-Part Conceptual Title

Do not invent cute puns or repeat the assignment wording. Build a title that previews the essay's central conceptual vocabulary:

1. Pull 6-7 prominent conceptual keywords from the **conclusion's main claim** (especially words that were *not* in the original prompt).
2. Assemble 3 candidate two-part titles on the model `Concept Anchor: Specific Tension or Angle` (first part ends in a colon).
3. Verify that every major noun in the chosen title actually plays a starring role inside the essay.

## Deliverable

Present the revised introduction, revised conclusion, and 3 ranked two-part title candidates (with the recommended pick highlighted).
