# Playbook: nuance

Calibrate the voice of the essay away from both strident overstatement and timid hedging, and strengthen credibility by confronting plausible counterevidence.

Load `references/calibration-examples.md` for the three-way tone calibration anchor (strident vs. mealy-mouthed vs. calibrated).

## Workflow

1. **Tame Strident Certainty**:
   - Find sweeping absolutes (`every`, `always`, `never`, `obviously`, `no one disputes`, `completely eliminated`) and replace them with precise scope boundaries (specify *which* group, *under what conditions*, *over what timeframe*, or *what specific kind* of phenomenon).
   - Precision beats vague softening: instead of turning "censorship vanished" into "censorship sort of almost vanished", specify "overt prior restraint by central courts largely vanished".

2. **Cut Mealy-Mouthed Hedge Stacking**:
   - Find sentences burdened by stacked qualifiers (`almost`, `fairly`, `somewhat`, `just about`, `arguably`, `seems to sort of`).
   - Strip redundant hedges until the sentence states a clear, accurately bounded claim with calm authority.

3. **Stress-Test with Plausible Counterevidence**:
   - Ask: *"What fact, passage, benchmark, or alternative interpretation in the same domain could a sharp critic cite against this claim?"*
   - Integrate 1-2 substantive acknowledgments/rebuttals where the argument is most vulnerable:
     - **Acknowledge + Rebut**: Show why the counterevidence does not actually overturn the thesis.
     - **Acknowledge + Qualify**: Narrow the claim's scope honestly so it remains defensible.
     - **Acknowledge + Weigh**: Concede the counter-point exists, then demonstrate why the balance of evidence still favors your claim.

## Deliverable

Apply tone calibration and counterevidence blocks directly, highlighting where scope was sharpened and what specific objection is now addressed.
